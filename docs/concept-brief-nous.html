A field test for the people who set up Hermes for a living

Search Fiverr for a Hermes setup and you'll find sellers offering to install it on your server. Nothing on the listing tells a buyer which of them can keep it running once a key leaks or the scheduler stops. Field Academy is a working Hermes Desktop plugin that teaches the job, then tests it on client machines we've broken on purpose.

What it is

A trail map inside Hermes Desktop

Field Academy installs as a plugin and opens as its own page in the app. A learner picks a trail, climbs three camps from Beginner to Advanced, and ends at a summit. The trails take their names from Hermes's old jobs on Mount Olympus: Messenger (explain Hermes and spot where it fits), Traveler (run Hermes on your own computer), Crossroads (connect Hermes to a client's tools), Boundary Keeper (who owns what, who approves, how it recovers) and Guide (deploy and run Hermes for a client). A new learner starts with Messenger's first mission: give Hermes one small real job and write down what it did and what it got wrong.

Twenty missions across the four learning trails, each in six steps: the story, a three-line takeaway, a half-done example to finish, a try in their own Hermes, an explanation in their own words, and a check. Practice prompts and scoring open a new chat and send themselves, and reading and video links wait at the end of each mission under Go deeper.

The check is the part that matters. It reads real work on the learner's machine, like a scheduled job that has actually run or a document with every section filled in, and it can't be ticked by hand.

The lessons link out to the people already teaching Hermes well: Tonbi's masterclass, Kevin Simback's Hermes Atlas and the Hermes Bible community.

The proposed credential

The Guide trail: four camps, nine parts, one signature

The trail follows the order a working consultant uses: look at what's broken, decide the boundaries, build one real thing, then prove it survives. Each part below is graded on what the learner did, never on what they say they did.

Field report · Case 4100

The key in the open

"The company that runs our AI account sent us an alert about strange traffic on our key."

Client
Birchwood Dental Partners, a fictional practice. The owner wants it fixed and written down, because the insurance form asks.
What they get
A folder of 18 files standing in for the client's machine: configuration, logs, a backup script and a key that shouldn't be where it is. Every key in it is made up.
What they hand in
The repaired files, an incident note saying what leaked, when, who was told and what stops it happening again, and a diagnosis in their own words.
How it's graded
Scripts check the folder against criteria the learner never sees. Deleting the broken file fails. A half-fix fails. A repair copied from someone else fails, because each learner's case is seeded differently.
When they miss
They're told what was missing and given one hint. A second miss shows a worked example. They can retry as often as they like.

Three more labs follow it: a scheduled job running every minute on an expensive model, an office machine that doesn't come back after a power cut, and one shared key doing the work of five.

How it teaches

Built on what the research says makes learning stick

Review
Spaced recall

Passed missions come back at two days, a week, three weeks and two months. A miss returns the next day.

Support
Worked examples that fade

A finished example first, then a half-done one, then the learner alone.

Feedback
A ladder after a miss

What was missing, then a hint, then a worked example. Never just "not yet."

Placement
Skip what you know

A short check lets experienced people skip a level. It never skips the proof of real work.

Transfer
Your own problem

Each trail ends with a capstone on the learner's own work, graded against criteria they write.

The sources for each choice are listed in the plugin's notes. The review timings and pass marks are my own settings, and I've marked which citations I couldn't confirm.

The summit

A certificate that shows its working

The certificate lists the proof behind it: each lab's case number and the fingerprint of the repaired folder, the proposal's score, the drills and what Hermes's own records showed.

It records the Hermes version it was earned on and asks for a re-check when the series changes, because a skill learned on one release can go stale on the next.

It can't be fully issued by software alone. A named reviewer reads the learner's packet and signs it. Until then the learner holds "Guide, awaiting review."

What I can't claim yet

A prototype, tested by its own test suite and not yet by learners

No learner has taken it. I don't know yet whether it teaches better than reading the docs.

It hasn't run on a live Hermes in this version. The labs imitate a Hermes home, and I haven't confirmed where Hermes keeps its run history.

There's no reviewer yet. The published reviewer list is empty, so no certificate can be fully issued.

It isn't a Nous credential, and every certificate says so on its face. The 627 automated tests prove the plugin behaves as designed, nothing more.

What I'm asking Nous for

Three things would turn this into something people can trust

  1. A view on whether a practitioner credential should exist

    And, if it should, who ought to stand behind it.

  2. A decision on who would review, if anyone

    No reviewers have been appointed or approached. If the Guide certificate should exist, who reviews the rubric and signs is Nous's call.

  3. Two facts about Hermes

    How releases are numbered, and where run history lives, so the evidence checks rest on fact.
